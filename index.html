One knowledge point, five moves

  1. Predict (optional). Pause and commit a number, a trend or a sentence; nothing is locked.
  2. See the mechanism. A widget whose control is the quantity the question varies, a stepped animation with one thing to notice, a worked derivation whose last step you write yourself the second time.
  3. Changed case. Answer a new case, closed book if you choose to test yourself.
  4. Retrieve later. Core cards come back on an FSRS schedule; detail cards (identifiers, exact constants) wait until the core is remembered twice.
  5. Provenance. Every claim is anchored to a line of the lecture code, a slide page, a paper section or a timestamp in the lecture video.

Two lines meet at the knowledge point

Teacher line: recover what the lecture rests on and what it filtered out. Discover tools read the executable lectures, slides and papers; human-made captions add 404 moments where the professor skipped, deferred or digressed, and 42 knowledge points that exist only in the audio.

Student line: decide what a learner needs to see. Animate only change over time; make a widget's numbers agree with the question; show the worked steps, then fade them.

GRPO's three policies

π_old resets every step; π_ref moves only at an iteration boundary, after the reward model is retrained.

What building it found

In the material

  • RefinedWeb's "600B of 5T" is a released share, not a filter rate.
  • FlashAttention's printed loop order is the reverse of the common lecture animation.
  • FeynRL says a temperature shift moves ESS, but its Eq. 11 is invariant to a common scale.
  • Chinchilla's Table A3 prints 1.26e24 FLOPs for the 175B row; 6ND gives 1.26e25.
  • 38 slips in the lectures (wrong stage numbers, swapped Q/K/V) recorded and not taught.

In our own design

  • A widget can compute the changed case, so the changed case became closed book.
  • Card titles stated the answer on 24% of review cards; reviews now hide them.
  • "Faded" steps hid nothing; worked steps are now real and the last one is yours to write.
  • About 50 older presentations described things they did not draw, or opened on the asked case.

Status. No learner has used it yet; every number above describes the material, not learning outcomes. A first study session is the next step.
